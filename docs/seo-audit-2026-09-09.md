# SEO audit, the two live Genesis domains

Date: 9 September 2026. Desk: web operations (Sebastian Moore, front of house and
copy; Felix Ward, infrastructure and data).

Scope is the two properties that have a purchased domain and are serving live
traffic:

| Site | Domain | Platform | Pages crawled |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Genesis Model Management | genesismodelmgmt.co.uk | Lovable, TanStack Start SSR | 198 in sitemap, 127 indexable |
| The Private Collection | theprivatecollection.org | Lovable, TanStack Start SSR | 84 |

Every figure below was taken from a live crawl of the published sites on the
audit date. Nothing is estimated. The raw crawl captured, per URL, the HTTP
status, title, meta description, canonical, robots directive, heading tree,
Open Graph tags, JSON-LD types, image alt coverage, response time and HTML
weight.

## Baseline: both sites were already in good order

This was not a neglected estate, and the audit is mostly about a small number of
real defects rather than a rebuild. Across both sites, before any change:

- no missing page titles, meta descriptions, canonical tags or `og:image` tags
- no duplicate titles and no duplicate descriptions
- no images missing an `alt` attribute, on any page of either site
- valid `robots.txt` and valid XML sitemaps, both referenced correctly
- one `<h1>` per page, with one exception noted below
- substantial and correct structured data on both sites

The Private Collection carries `Product`, `Brand` and `ImageObject` on 67 piece
pages, `BreadcrumbList` on 80, plus `FAQPage`, `ItemList`, `Organization`,
`WebSite` and four `Article` entries. Genesis carries `Person` and `ProfilePage`
on 112 model profiles, `BreadcrumbList` on 126, plus `LocalBusiness`,
`Organization`, `CollectionPage`, `AboutPage`, `ContactPage`, `Service` and a
`SearchAction`. Both declare `en-GB`; The Private Collection also serves
`hreflang` alternates including `x-default`.

## Genesis Model Management

### 1. The sitemap advertises 71 pages that are marked noindex (high)

71 of the 198 URLs in `sitemap.xml`, 36 per cent of the file, are sports
division records at `/talent/<slug>`. Every one of them serves
`<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">`.

The cause is two rules that disagree. `src/routes/sitemap[.]xml.ts` filtered
talent entries with `isPublic(m)`, which only checks that the record is not
quarantined and has a usable name. `src/routes/talent.$slug.tsx` marks a profile
noindex when `isSports || m.anon === true || /^gsx-/i.test(slug)`. Sports
records satisfy the first and fail the second, so they were published in the
sitemap and suppressed on the page.

Both files already stated the intended behaviour in their own comments. The
sitemap said anonymised records "are excluded by isPublic"; the profile route
said they "stay out of the index and out of the sitemap". Neither was true.

Search consequence: Google Search Console reports these as "Submitted URL marked
noindex", an error state, and more than a third of the crawl budget for the
sitemap is spent on pages that can never rank.

There is a second consequence that matters more than the ranking one. The
sitemap is a public file, and the athlete slugs are full real names, while the
pages themselves deliberately show only an abbreviated label and describe
themselves as anonymised sports profiles. The sitemap therefore published, in
one machine-readable list, the full roster of names that the pages take care not
to show. The sports records are not linked from `/sports` at all, so the sitemap
was the only thing making them enumerable.

Fix applied: a single exported predicate, `isIndexableProfile`, now decides
both the sitemap entry and the page's robots directive, so the two cannot drift
apart again. This mirrors how `src/lib/robots.ts` already keeps the indexing and
AI directives in one place. The 112 women's and men's profiles are unaffected,
and the sports pages keep rendering exactly as they do now, still noindex and
still reachable by direct URL.

### 2. Unknown URLs return HTTP 200 instead of 404, catch-all only (medium)

`https://genesismodelmgmt.co.uk/anything-that-does-not-exist` returned
`HTTP/2 200`. The `/$` catch-all rendered the correct not-found page with the
correct `noindex, follow` directive but never set the response status.

This is confined to the catch-all. `/talent/<unknown-slug>` already returned a
correct 404, because its loader throws `notFound()`, and was left alone.

Severity is medium rather than high because the noindex directive does stop the
pages entering the index. What it costs is a Search Console soft 404 report on
every mistyped or stale inbound link, and silent failure for external link
checkers and monitoring. The Private Collection returns a correct 404 for the
same test, so the two sites were inconsistent, as were the two routes within
Genesis itself.

Fix applied: the catch-all now throws `notFound()`, the same mechanism the
talent route already used successfully, so the server answers 404. Markup and
copy are unchanged.

One caveat carried into review. The change also removed the `head()` block from
`$.tsx`, on the grounds that the root's `notFoundComponent` renders the same
title, description and `noindex` directive. That is what the source says, but
both files carry comments recording an earlier bug in which two competing
`robots` metas were deduplicated and the wrong one survived. Since a 404 page
that loses its `noindex` is worse than the soft 404 being fixed, this has been
sent back for verification against the rendered HTML rather than accepted from
the code alone.

### 3. Slow server response and heavy HTML (medium, not yet actioned)

Measured response times on the live site, single sequential fetches:

| URL | Time | HTML |
| --- | --- | --- |
| /sports | 6.63s | 267 KB |
| /join-us | 5.65s | 196 KB |
| /industrynews | 4.77s | 327 KB |
| / | 4.99s | 207 KB |
| /women | 4.89s | 262 KB |
| /men | (4.5s band) | 261 KB |

For comparison, The Private Collection answers in 0.9 to 1.5 seconds. HTML is
served gzipped, not brotli, and `cache-control` on documents is
`no-cache, must-revalidate, max-age=0`, so no CDN edge caching absorbs the cost.
This is the single largest remaining opportunity on the site and it bears
directly on Core Web Vitals and on crawl rate.

This has not been changed. A speculative performance refactor on a live site is
the wrong move without first measuring where the time goes. Recommended next
step is to establish whether the cost sits in the First Option roster feed call
made per request, in the volume of server rendered markup, or in cold starts,
then act on the finding.

### 4. `Disallow: /talent/gsx-` protects nothing (low)

`robots.txt` carries the rule under the comment "Anonymised sports records are
enquiry gates". No live URL uses the `gsx-` prefix; the athlete records sit at
`/talent/<real-name>`. The rule matches zero URLs and offers false assurance.

Deliberately not changed. Broadening it to cover the athlete pages would be the
wrong fix: a `Disallow` prevents crawlers from fetching the page, which prevents
them from ever seeing the `noindex` on it, and a blocked URL that is linked from
anywhere can still be indexed on the strength of its URL alone. The correct
combination for these pages is what is now in place, crawlable and noindex, with
no sitemap entry. Removing the dead line is a tidy-up for Steven to approve.

### 5. `max-image-preview:standard` (recommendation, needs a decision)

Every indexable page sends
`index, follow, max-image-preview:standard, noarchive, noai, noimageai, noml`.

`max-image-preview:large` is what allows a full size thumbnail in Google Images
and makes a page eligible for Discover. For a model agency, image search is a
material discovery channel, so `standard` is leaving reach on the table.

This has deliberately not been changed. It sits next to `noai`, `noimageai` and
`noml`, and `robots.txt` blocks roughly thirty AI and scraper agents, so the
restrictive image setting looks like a considered rights and safeguarding
position on model photography rather than an oversight. Changing how model
imagery is exposed is Steven's call, not the desk's. The trade is: `large`
increases image search visibility, and does not weaken the AI training
reservations, which are carried by separate directives.

## The Private Collection

### 6. `/journal` had no `<h1>` (medium)

The journal index rendered "Notes for the collector." as an `<h2>` and had no
`<h1>` at all. It was the only page on either site missing one. The four article
headings below it are correctly `<h2>`.

Fix applied: promoted to the page's single `<h1>` with rendering unchanged.

### 7. Six titles truncated in search results (medium)

Twelve titles exceeded 62 characters; six were long enough to lose their tail in
a results page.

| URL | Was | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| /journal/box-and-papers-full-set | 93 | 51 |
| /journal/how-private-watch-acquisition-works | 86 | 54 |
| /sell-your-watch | 83 | 49 |
| /journal/authenticating-a-modern-grail | 83 | 54 |
| /approach | 80 | 61 |
| /journal/sourcing-a-watch-you-cannot-find | 75 | 59 |

The four journal entries needed a second pass. The first attempt shortened the
`title` field in `src/lib/journal.ts`, which is not only the `<title>` tag: it
also renders the visible `<h1>`, the cards on `/journal` and in "More from the
Journal", `og:title`, the `Article` schema `headline` and the breadcrumb name.
Shortening it therefore rewrote the site's visible editorial headlines, and into
Title Case, which does not match the house sentence-case voice. That was an
error in how the change was specified, not in how it was carried out.

Corrected by separating the two concerns, which is the right pattern regardless:
the editorial headline stays in `title` exactly as written, and a new optional
`metaTitle` feeds the `<title>` tag alone. The on-page copy is unchanged.

The brand pages at 63 and 64 characters and the `/piece/` titles at 66 to 70
were left alone on purpose. They are only marginally over, and the reference
names and brand keywords in them earn the length. Trimming to hit a character
count is not worth the churn.

### 8. Three meta descriptions over 160 characters (low)

`/richard-mille` at 172, `/sell-your-watch` at 169 and `/sourcing` at 169 were
losing their closing clause. All three rewritten under 150 characters, keeping
the brand keywords and the "price on application" framing.

### 9. Product schema has no `offers`, and should stay that way (noted, not a defect to fix)

The 67 `/piece/` pages carry well-formed `Product` markup: name, brand,
manufacturer, model, mpn, sku, category, material, `itemCondition`
`UsedCondition`, and an `ImageObject` with a real caption. It omits `offers`.

Google Search Console will report "Missing field offers" as a **warning** on
these pages. The pages still index normally; what they forfeit is eligibility
for price-bearing merchant rich results.

This is recorded here specifically so that nobody later "fixes" the warning by
inventing a price. The business is price on application, the repository enforces
that with `scripts/check-no-prices.mjs`, and putting a placeholder or a zero
price into `offers` to clear a Search Console warning would publish a false
commercial term. The warning is the correct trade and should be left in place.

If it is ever worth closing, the only honest route is an `Offer` carrying
availability and a `businessFunction` with no price, which does not fully
satisfy the merchant listing requirements either. Accepting the warning is
simpler and more truthful.

### 9. Legal pages send a different robots directive (low, not actioned)

`/privacy`, `/terms` and `/cookies` send `index, follow` while the other 81
pages send `index, follow, max-image-preview:large`. Harmless, since those pages
carry no imagery worth previewing, but it is an inconsistency worth collapsing
next time that file is touched.

## What was changed and what was not

Changed, as technical hygiene inside the Phase A envelope of sitemap and robots
hygiene plus meta titles and descriptions:

- Genesis: one shared `isIndexableProfile` predicate driving both the sitemap
  and the profile robots directive, removing 71 noindex URLs from the sitemap
- Genesis: real HTTP 404 on the catch-all route, pending the head-metadata
  verification noted above
- The Private Collection: `/journal` `<h1>`, six titles, three descriptions

Not changed, and needing Steven:

- the `max-image-preview` setting, because it touches model imagery policy
- the dead `Disallow: /talent/gsx-` line
- server response time and HTML weight on Genesis, pending a diagnosis
- the legal pages' robots directive

No copy about a real person, a rate or a client was rewritten. No credential was
entered anywhere.
